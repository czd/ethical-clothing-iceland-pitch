Evidence & assumptions / 27 October 2026

The story, with its workings.

A proposal for affordable, fair, traceable clothing in Iceland. The presentation is a vision and a test design—not a claim that the business, suppliers or demand are already validated.

All garment imagery is original concept illustration, not a photograph of verified stock. All venture prices, costs, quantities, test periods and partnership ideas are illustrative or proposed. No orders, supplier agreements, wage outcomes, exclusive arrangements or sales uplift are established.

Slides 04 / 06 / 07

Follow one shirt. Keep the unknowns visible.

The chain and display are design proposals, not verified product provenance. A selected batch should link fibre → yarn/fabric/processing → cutting/sewing/finishing → import → use/repair. Name the known facilities and dates, identify the evidence and its scope, and state missing stages plainly.

  • Independent evidence: name who checked what, when and with what limitations.
  • Brand/supplier reported: do not silently present self-report as independent verification.
  • Estimated: explain the allocation and input assumptions.
  • Unknown: make gaps as readable as positive claims.

A supplier invoice includes material, labour, overhead and margin; it is not worker pay. A material or facility certificate alone does not establish normal-hours living wages for every worker. Direct sourcing does not itself establish fairness. Publish no worker identity, payroll or sensitive grievance information.

Open Supply Hub offers an existing facility-identity reference, not product chain-of-custody or wage certification. Its methodology is a reference for a later evidence process, not evidence for this concept garment.

The presentation is an English discussion document. A live Iceland-targeted shop needs reviewed Icelandic advertising/terms, total delivered prices, composition/fit information, lawful withdrawal/refund arrangements, product/import compliance, privacy and payment checks. The research did not provide launch clearance. Ordinary online garments generally carry withdrawal rights; fit-related returns cannot be wished away.

Slide 08

Measure the presentation. Don’t invent the uplift.

Proposed comparison: A shows standard product, fit, price and required information. B keeps those constant and adds an evidence-led visual display / follow-the-shirt explanation. Never withhold legally required facts from the control.

  1. Define eligible exposure, one primary metric and a minimum useful commercial effect before starting. If online traffic supports it, randomise eligible visitors. With a pop-up, use balanced scheduled blocks and log staffing, time, weather and traffic differences; interpret that as weaker causal evidence.
  2. Hold product, price, stock availability, channel and traffic mix as comparable as possible. Log evidence engagement separately from purchases. Collect only necessary, lawfully handled measurement data.
  3. Conversion = orders / eligible visits. Basket = ex-VAT net retained sales / retained orders; also track retained items/order and channel. Returns = returned orders / dispatched orders for a defined mature cohort, with partial returns tracked separately.
  4. Report contribution both per retained order and per eligible visit after refunds and variable costs. Include fulfilment, fees, return handling/impairment and acquisition. Record staff/time differences rather than treating extra service as free.
  5. Review only after the relevant return windows and processing lag. Report denominators and uncertainty. A small six-week pilot may be directional learning, not a statistically powered A/B result. Do not declare a winner from clicks or a few orders.

Success gates and sample size are to be agreed with the retail partner before a test. No uplift, conversion rate, retention or willingness-to-pay result is claimed.

Contribution is not profit. It still has to fund fixed staff/founder pay, rent, recurring systems, bookkeeping, verification overhead and finance; profit tax is also outside this figure. Delivery sensitivity

Holding other inputs constant: charging 990 delivery gives 980.20; no delivery charge gives 210.93 (payment fee falls with gross collected); pickup with no delivery charge or carrier cost gives 1,269.75. Pickup still needs premises and staff funding. Multi-item basket benefits depend on actual delivery costs and partial returns, and are untested.

Cash, scale and adverse cases

Scenario B stock requires 500 × 1,785 = 892,500 ex-VAT inventory cost. Add 209,400 import VAT and 4,800 assumed domestic-clearance VAT: 1,106,700 acquisition cash before selling. This is not total startup capital. Samples, setup, verification, refunds, fulfilment, wages, premises and contingencies are additional.

At an illustrative fixed-cost budget of ISK 1m/month, 980.198292 contribution would require 1,021 retained one-item orders/month before finance and profit tax. Neither that budget nor that demand is researched. Free delivery is not automatically funded by larger volume.

One-change sensitivities, rounded ISK: 30% returns → 512 contribution; acquisition 1,000 instead of 400 → 380; EUR goods/freight 10% more expensive → 805. No end-of-season unsold stock loss is included in those figures.

Slides 12–14

A cap, a learning question, a way to stop.

Proposed pilot: at most 100 units of one garment, one or two colours and a deliberate size curve, sourced as new existing stock bought wholesale rather than commissioning a private-label production run. “Resale” here does not necessarily mean secondhand.

  1. Before spending: agree the target shopper, sample/fit criteria, evidence floor, actual quote, VAT/import treatment, full cash budget and numeric loss cap. These are not yet approved. Do not assume ≤100 units will achieve the 500-unit consolidated-case costs.
  2. Before selling: substantiate all product claims; complete legal, product, language, privacy, tax, checkout/refund and merchant checks. Confirm wholesale/reseller rights and stock availability.
  3. Proposed six-week selling test: use a limited local display/pickup arrangement without a lease commitment; ordinary online content can support it. No automatic preorder or future availability promise. Any venue or provider terms must be separately approved.
  4. After returns mature: reconcile kept revenue, contribution, refunds, markdowns and stock remaining. Reorder only if actual economics, evidence and customer response pass agreed gates; revise or stop otherwise.

The retail/creative and technology/operations contributions are ideas for a discussion. No person is assigned a title, workload, equity, compensation or liability. Start with a shared experiment and agree how to decide together.
